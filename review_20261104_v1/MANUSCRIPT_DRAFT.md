# English manuscript text — exploratory application

These paragraphs describe the observed-state application, not a test of the trained packer. Original registered endpoints retain their original status; all additional rank, subgroup, interaction and structure analyses are exploratory.

## Results

To assess whether crystallographically modelled side-chain alternatives can aid ligand-pose recovery, we compared single-state and multistate receptors on apo and holo backbones. Receptors were constructed from qFit models without a learned packer, with at most eight retained combinations on a fixed major backbone. The main analysed cohort comprised 128 cases in 39 homology components. Each arm received 50 Vina docking calls, with the multistate arm sharing this budget across its receptors. Paired improvements were defined as single-state minus multistate RMSD and averaged first within components and then across components. Neither registered co-primary endpoint met its improvement criterion: the component-mean gain was −0.017 Å for top-1 RMSD (Bonferroni-adjusted 97.5% interval, −0.306 to +0.318 Å) and +0.192 Å for the best of five geometrically distinct score-ranked poses (−0.144 to +0.545 Å). These intervals do not establish equivalence.

The secondary oracle endpoint nevertheless indicated improved availability of near-native poses. The equal-component mean oracle gain was +0.326 Å (95% interval, +0.123 to +0.580 Å). A pose within 2 Å was generated in 60 single-state and 68 multistate cases, comprising nine success gains across six components and one loss. Top-1 success remained 18 cases in each arm, with two gains and two losses; distinct top-5 success increased from 36 to 40 cases, with six gains and two losses. The initial pilot and expanded budgets are nested analyses rather than independent replications. In an exploratory interaction analysis, the oracle gain was greater on apo than holo backbones by +0.334 Å (95% interval, +0.132 to +0.572 Å), whereas the top-1 interaction remained unresolved (−0.120 Å; −0.479 to +0.248 Å).

We reanalysed retrieval as the score rank of the first pose within 2 Å, conditional on at least one such pose being present. Its median Vina rank was 62.5 for single-state and 40.5 for multistate apo receptors; respectively, 68 and 60 cases contained no qualifying pose. A random ordering must account for both the total candidate count N and qualifying count G, giving an expected first-success rank of (N+1)/(G+1). Relative to this conditional null, Vina retrieval was heterogeneous: some cases selected a correct pose immediately, whereas others placed the first correct pose substantially later than expected. Rescoring the same 50-call pose pools with GNINA CNNscore retrieved near-native poses earlier, but the multistate top-1 RMSD gain remained uncertain (+0.126 Å; 95% interval, −0.185 to +0.489 Å). The separate eight-call GNINA docking run used its default empirical search followed by CNN rescoring, not CNN-guided search; its paired top-1 gain was −0.280 Å (−0.931 to +0.274 Å).

Exploratory inspection of all ten oracle-success switches identified plausible structural contributions in some cases. For example, alternative Met237 in two 6-nitrobenzotriazole cases relieved severe contacts between the major-state receptor and the successful ligand pose, while Ile621 in another case approached the holo major rotamer. Other successful combinations showed no severe-clash relief or unambiguous holo-state match. Successful multistate poses occurred in 4–23 existing calls per gained case. These repeated outcomes and matched-seed major-state controls support receptor-dependent changes in the tested searches, but do not establish that experimentally modelled alternatives are superior to generic receptor diversity.

## Discussion

This application illustrates why candidate-state coverage and final prediction accuracy should be evaluated separately. The observed-state ensembles improved the best pose available to the tested Vina workflow without establishing improvement of its selected pose. This parallels, but does not directly validate, the parent study's distinction between distributional agreement and minor-state identification: a broader set of useful candidates and the ability to identify a useful candidate are different properties. The application used observed qFit alternatives rather than the occupancy-trained packer, so it does not show that the packer's measured discrimination gain transfers to docking.

The result should not be interpreted as an information ceiling for qFit states or for side-chain prediction. The ensembles retained at most eight independently constructed combinations, fixed the major backbone, applied clash filtering and divided a fixed search budget among receptors. No-good-pose cases remained common, and the GNINA docking run did not eliminate the difference between empirical generation and CNN selection. Possible training exposure may affect CNN scores differently across receptor states and cannot be assumed to cancel in a paired comparison. Moreover, the present comparison does not distinguish state-specific information from the general value of conformational diversity. The most direct next test is therefore an ensemble drawn from a backbone-conditioned rotamer prior with matched receptor numbers, preparation and docking budgets. Such a control would test whether the locations of the observed alternatives add useful information beyond an equally sized set of plausible alternatives.
